GitHub announced Project HydraFusion on 4 September 2026 as a Copilot CLI research preview. It chooses a single model, a cascade that escalates after a quality gate, or a cross-family read-only critique followed by one revision. The vendor says it is available through /experimental, bills the token use of constituent models, and is best suited today to first-turn, single-prompt coding tasks; longer multi-turn behavior remains under development. Across its offline comparison with Claude Opus 5, GitHub reports: TerminalBench 2.1 +4.9 verified-quality points at 67% lower estimated cost; DeepSWE -1.5 points at 36% lower cost; CheckpointBench -0.1 points at 65% lower cost. GitHub states that the model roster and behavior may change during the preview. Independent coverage notes that cost falls across all three reported tests while quality matches or exceeds the Opus baseline only on one.
